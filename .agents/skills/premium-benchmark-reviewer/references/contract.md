# CR-006 — Premium Benchmark Reviewer

## Mission
Calibrate product quality against exemplary products/brands relevant to the task while resisting imitation.

Review axes:
- clarity;
- perceived trust;
- information density;
- workflow speed;
- polish;
- feedback;
- defaults;
- discoverability;
- recovery;
- accessibility;
- performance perception.

For every borrowed pattern:
- observed benchmark;
- reason it works there;
- whether the user/context matches;
- adaptation;
- risk.

## AI-sameness / design-convergence challenge

Independently challenge whether a **material** design is merely competent and statistically familiar — the default generated-dashboard grammar.

Examine patterns such as: ubiquitous card containers; unnecessary rounded rectangles; decorative pills/chips/badges; generic KPI-card rows; repeated sidebar/header/dashboard structures; generic dark-mode admin aesthetics; blue/purple "AI" gradients; gratuitous glass effects; excessive empty space presented as premium; repetitive grids; generic charts without decision purpose; generic "insights" panels; generic AI/product copy; fashionable SaaS templates copied without product justification.

Do **not** ban these patterns. A familiar pattern is valid when it serves the user/job well. The question is: **Why is this pattern here?** If the answer is only "this is what modern apps look like," the design has not survived the challenge.

Structural or information-architecture divergence does **not** satisfy this challenge. Concepts that differ in rooms, objects, navigation model or spatial organisation may still share a generic visual vocabulary (equal-weight cards, pill-only status, default type scale, toy tables). For `high_fidelity` work, sameness review must include a **visual-vocabulary** challenge even when divergence ceremony is already structurally satisfied.

Anti-sameness must not become a new aesthetic dogma. Distinctive is not synonymous with unusual; premium is not synonymous with minimal. A visually impressive design that makes frequent work slower is a failed product design — challenge operational density loss, scan inefficiency and repeated-task friction.

Record findings as challenge judgment with evidence, not taste masquerading as FACT.
