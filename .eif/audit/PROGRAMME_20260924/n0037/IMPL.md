## Threat note
Attacker goals, and what this change does about each:
- **Online password guessing against one account.** Mitigated: at most 5 wrong guesses per 15 minutes per account, whatever the source address (roughly 480 a day, down from unbounded).
- **Password spraying (one guess against many accounts from one address).** Partly mitigated: 20 failures per 15 minutes per address. This can be bypassed by a client that sets its own XFF through the Next proxy (criterion 6) or by an attacker with many addresses. A successful login deliberately does not clear the address counter, so an attacker can't use a valid account of their own to reset it.
- **Account enumeration.** Mitigated: status and body are the same for unknown, inactive and wrong-password; PBKDF2 cost is the same; lockout behaves the same for existing and non-existent emails.
- **Lockout DoS against a known email.** NOT mitigated, and made possible by this change: anyone who knows an email can keep that account locked with 5 bad attempts every 15 minutes. This is the accepted trade-off of per-account lockout. Possible later options: an admin unlock, a CAPTCHA or back-off in place of a hard lock, or exempting the address of the last successful login.
- **Remaining gaps.** Counters are in memory and per process: they reset on API restart (including every `--reload`) and are not shared if the API ever runs more than one worker or replica. That would need a shared store such as Redis, with a safe fallback when it is down. Concurrent requests fired before the first failure is recorded can go a few attempts over the limit (check and record are separated by the DB lookup); later attempts are still blocked. The tracked-key map is capped at 10,000 keys (expired keys are swept, then the oldest are evicted) to bound memory under email spraying; eviction under a flood can forget a lock early. The Retry-After value reveals nothing about whether an account exists.
